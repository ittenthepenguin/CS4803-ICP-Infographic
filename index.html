Ideal Customer Profile and Key Stakeholders

Working concept: preserving and transferring commercial-fleet incident video so the people handling a claim can find, understand, and assess it. We are testing whether this is a recurring, costly problem; an API or other product is not yet a decision.

01

The starting point

1. Initial Ideal Customer Profile

Our initial customer hypothesis is a U.S. commercial fleet with a centralized safety or risk function, dashcams already in use, and a recurring need to provide incident evidence to an insurer or third-party administrator (TPA). This is an organizational profile, not a claim that all fleets share the same pain. We will screen for a named safety/risk owner, multiple vehicles, an existing camera or telematics provider, and a documented path for reporting incidents.

The proposed economic buyer is a director or vice president responsible for fleet risk, safety, or claims outcomes. A safety analyst or claims coordinator may retrieve footage and assemble the incident package. Fleet IT and security may approve access to camera data. These roles are hypotheses to verify, especially whether the fleet, insurer, or TPA actually pays for improvements.

Motivations and behavior to test

We expect this segment to value timely incident handling, reliable evidence, driver fairness, and predictable risk costs. We do not yet know how strongly any of these motives shape purchasing. In interviews, we will ask for the most recent incident, who requested footage, how it was retrieved and shared, what information accompanied it, and what happened if a clip was late or unavailable.

Existing tools matter. Motive documents an asynchronous video-recall API with clip limits and request quotas (Motive API). Guidewire describes claim-related media as documents and provides document endpoints (Guidewire documentation). These sources show technical routes exist; they do not show how often a real fleet or TPA has a painful handoff. That frequency is the central discovery question.

02

Choosing who to learn from first

2. Why Start With Fleets?

FLEET
CLAIMS

A fleet controls the incident response and the source of its camera footage, so its safety/risk team can recount concrete retrieval and sharing episodes. That makes it a practical first interview segment for a small student team. TPAs remain a comparison segment because they receive the evidence and may bear more of the processing cost. We are not assuming a fleet has the budget or authority to buy software; interviews must establish who experiences the loss, who approves a change, and whether existing tools suffice.

There is a specific but limited policy signal: Louisiana requires qualifying commercial-auto insurers to offer an actuarially justified liability-premium discount for vehicles with a qualifying dashboard camera and telematics system (Louisiana statute). This is a state-specific incentive, not a camera mandate or proof that video transfer is broken. It strengthens the case for asking fleets how camera data affects their insurance relationship.

03

The people around the handoff

3. Stakeholder Map

A simple map of the groups that can use, enable, constrain, or be affected by a future solution.

01

Fleet safety analyst and IT/security team - high influence

The analyst is the likely daily operator; IT/security can permit or block data access. They care about effort, data handling, and fitting existing systems. We need to verify who performs each step rather than assuming one role does both.

02

TPA or insurer claims team - high influence

Adjusters and claims leaders receive the evidence and decide whether it is usable in their workflow. They may be users, buyers, or blockers depending on who owns the process and budget. Guidewire's documented claim-document API and CCC's developer program show that claims-side integrations already exist in parts of this ecosystem; neither proves that a target TPA uses them for fleet video (Guidewire documentation; CCC Secure Share).

03

Camera and claims-platform providers - high technical influence

These providers control retrieval, permissions, formats, and integration terms. Their interest is use of their platforms and a manageable support burden. Their documented interfaces may enable a solution or make a new intermediary unnecessary; we must examine the actual tools customers use.

04

Defense counsel and fraud investigators - medium to high influence

They may set requirements for preserving a clip's origin and history. In federal proceedings, Evidence Rule 902(13)-(14) offers certification routes for certain electronic records and copies, with a qualified person's certification and notice requirements; it does not make a product's output automatically admissible (Federal Rule of Evidence 902). Their practical needs require interviews.

05

Drivers and labor representatives - medium influence

Drivers are affected by recording and by decisions based on footage. Privacy, fair treatment, and job security may shape acceptance. California Penal Code section 632 addresses recording confidential communications without all-party consent, which is relevant to some in-cab audio situations; it is not a blanket rule for road-facing video (California Penal Code section 632).

04

What must be true

4. Ranked Assumptions and Mission

First: needed footage or context is sometimes delayed, unavailable, or hard to transfer, with a consequence the fleet or claims team can describe from a real incident. Second: the problem recurs often enough that someone has tried a workaround or allocated staff time. Third: one identifiable organization owns the cost and can approve a better process. Fourth: existing camera and claims-platform tools leave a gap worth solving. Interviews may contradict any of these assumptions.

Our mission

Our mission is to help commercial-auto claims be decided with timely, trustworthy incident information. The opportunity is real only if the interviews identify a recurring handoff failure, a customer who bears its cost, and an improvement that current tools do not already provide. The initial ICP and stakeholder map tell us whom to interview and whose approval or cooperation a future solution would require.
