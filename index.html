01 / Initial customer

Start with a fleet safety or risk team

Hypothesis, not validation

A U.S. commercial fleet with centralized safety or risk ownership, dashcams already in use, and a recurring need to provide incident evidence to an insurer or third-party administrator (TPA).

A / Organization

Screening criteria

  • Multiple commercial vehicles
  • Named safety or risk owner
  • Existing camera or telematics provider
  • Documented incident-reporting path

Fleet size, industry, and location will be refined through interviews rather than invented as hard thresholds.

B / People

Likely roles

  • Proposed buyer: fleet risk, safety, or claims leader
  • Daily operator: safety analyst or claims coordinator
  • Gatekeeper: fleet IT and security

We still need to learn whether the fleet, insurer, or TPA controls budget and approval.

C / Mindset

Motivations to test

  • Timely incident handling
  • Reliable evidence and driver fairness
  • Predictable risk costs
  • Confidence in existing systems

These are interview prompts, not claims about what all fleets value.

D / Behavior

What we will observe

  • Recent footage requests and retrieval steps
  • Who checks and shares a clip
  • What context accompanies video
  • What happens when evidence is late or missing

We will also ask when the current process worked well.

Why this starting point?

The fleet controls the source.

Its team can describe a real incident from capture through handoff. Claims teams remain a comparison segment: they receive the material and may bear a different cost. Starting with fleets is a research tactic, not proof they will buy. A Louisiana camera discount law is a limited policy signal, not a camera mandate or proof of unmet demand.

01

Fleet safety analyst + IT/security

Likely operator and gatekeeper · High influence

The analyst may retrieve and package video; IT/security may allow or block data access. Both care about workload, secure handling, and fit with current systems. Verify: who actually owns each step?

02

TPA or insurer claims team

Recipient; possible buyer or blocker · High influence

Adjusters and claims leaders decide whether evidence is usable. They may own a different part of the cost. Existing claims APIs and partner programs mean we must ask what they already use.

03

Camera + claims-platform providers

Technical dependency · High influence

They control retrieval, permissions, formats, and integration terms. Their interfaces may enable a solution or make an intermediary unnecessary. Verify: the tools used by the target customer.

04

Defense counsel + fraud investigators

Evidence requirements · Medium to high influence

They may specify what must be preserved about a clip's origin and history. Federal Rule of Evidence 902 allows certain certification routes; software alone does not make footage admissible.

05

Drivers + labor representatives

Affected group · Medium influence

Recording and decisions based on footage affect privacy, fair treatment, and job security. California's section 632 can matter to confidential in-cab audio, not road-facing video as a blanket rule.

03 / Opportunity test

Four assumptions must survive interviews.

These are ranked by what we need to learn first. They are not findings.

  1. 01

    Needed footage or context is sometimes delayed, unavailable, or hard to transfer, with a consequence someone can describe from a real incident.

  2. 02

    The problem recurs often enough that a team has tried a workaround or allocated staff time.

  3. 03

    One identifiable organization bears the cost and can approve a better process.

  4. 04

    Existing camera and claims-platform tools leave a gap worth solving.

If interviews do not reveal a recurring failure, a buyer, and a meaningful gap, we should change the segment or the problem rather than force a product.

Evidence notes

What the sources do and do not show

Existing technical routes

Motive video-recall API, Guidewire claim-document API, and CCC claims-platform integration show that relevant tools exist. They do not establish how often a target customer's handoff fails.

Limited policy signal

Louisiana R.S. 22:1482.2 calls for a qualifying commercial-auto liability-premium discount. It does not require fleets to install cameras.

Legal boundaries

Federal Rule of Evidence 902(13)–(14) describes certification routes for certain electronic records and copies. California Penal Code § 632 concerns confidential communications. Neither proves a product requirement.
