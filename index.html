Ideal customer profile · Fall 2026

Meet the person who gets the call after a crash.

Our first customer is not “the transportation industry.” It is a commercial fleet’s safety and risk team: the people who must find the right video, explain where it came from, and get it to the claims team while it can still help.

01 / The customer

A fleet with cameras, a real safety function, and something at stake.

Because this is a business-to-business customer, the useful “demographics” are firmographics: what the organization looks like, how it works, and who owns the problem.

A / Firmographics

Who qualifies?

  • U.S.-based fleet operating across one or more states
  • Trucking, delivery, field service, utility, rental, or another road-intensive business
  • Enough vehicles and claim activity to support a named safety or risk owner
  • Dashcams or video telematics already installed
  • A repeatable process for reporting incidents to an insurer or TPA

A working screening range is 100–2,500 vehicles, but structure matters more than a hard vehicle count. Interviews must refine this range.

B / Psychographics

What do we expect to matter?

  • Evidence first. They want a defensible account, not a flashy dashboard.
  • Pragmatic about technology. They will use another tool only if it removes work from an existing incident process.
  • Protective of drivers. Video can support fair fault decisions, but inward-facing cameras and audio create trust concerns.
  • Alert to tail risk. One severe claim can matter more than many routine incidents.

These are beliefs to test in interviews, not findings about every fleet safety leader.

C / Behaviors

What do they do today?

  • Review alerts and reconstruct the time around an incident
  • Request a clip from the camera platform, sometimes after the vehicle reconnects
  • Match video with vehicle, driver, time, GPS, and other records
  • Send evidence through email, links, portals, or a claim system
  • Answer follow-up questions from adjusters, counsel, and internal leaders

The first two behaviors are technically plausible from vendor documentation; the complete sequence still needs to be observed in interviews (Motive, 2026; Samsara, n.d.).

D / Jobs, pains, and gains

Job to be done

Get the right evidence to the right decision-maker before it is lost, separated from its context, or questioned.

Pains to test

Offline vehicles, narrow recall windows, missing context, repeated uploads, uncertain custody, and incompatible intake rules.

Desired gain

A faster, calmer incident response in which the original clip and its history travel together.

03 / Research evidence

What the desk research changes.

The opportunity is not “getting video out of a camera.” That capability already exists. The open question is whether the trip between systems damages speed, context, or trust.

01

Retrieval exists, but it is vendor-specific.

Samsara describes an asynchronous request that waits for an offline device to reconnect and enforces monthly retrieval quotas. Motive limits regular recalls to one, two, or three minutes, limits smart-dashcam recalls to one minute, and rate-limits the endpoint. Lytx also publishes a Video API (Lytx, 2026; Motive, 2026; Samsara, n.d.).

Meaning for the ICP: Morgan’s problem, if it exists, begins after choosing the right vendor workflow—not because APIs are absent.

02

The claims side is not empty territory.

Guidewire treats claim media as “documents” and exposes document endpoints. Verisk already sells tools for detecting manipulation, duplication, and reuse in claim images and documents (Guidewire, 2024; Verisk, 2024).

Meaning for the ICP: a new venture has to complement or outperform products already inside the claim workflow.

03

Provenance has a standard, but standards do not create demand.

C2PA’s Content Credentials can bind tamper-evident provenance to media. Federal evidence rules also allow certain electronic records and copies to be authenticated by a qualified person’s certification. Neither fact proves that fleets or claims teams will pay for a new product (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity [C2PA], 2026; Federal Judicial Center, 2017).

Meaning for the ICP: ask counsel and claims teams what record they actually need.

04

Policy creates pressure, not proof.

Louisiana now requires qualifying commercial-auto insurers to offer an actuarially justified liability-premium discount for vehicles with an eligible dashcam and telematics system. The law rewards verified camera use; it does not mandate cameras or prove a broken evidence process (Louisiana State Legislature, 2025).

Meaning for the ICP: insurance incentives may help fleets care, but interviews must connect the incentive to workflow pain.

Important correction from the original draft: there is no defensible basis here for a $4.8B market claim, 180–260% ROI, a universal 24-hour video-retention rule, or the statement that insurers refuse to renew fleets without dashcams. Those claims are excluded.

04 / Why start with fleets?

The best first segment is the one we can learn from—and possibly serve.

TPAs may eventually feel more volume, but commercial fleets are a stronger starting point for customer discovery. They hold the source data, can recount a specific incident, and are easier for a student team to reach. Commercial auto also remained an unfavorable insurance line in 2025, with loss severity pressured by social inflation and third-party litigation financing (AM Best, 2026).

01 / Economic buyer

VP or Director of Fleet Risk / Safety

Interest
Loss outcomes, premium and deductible exposure, driver fairness, and a process the team can execute under pressure.
Influence
High. Likely sponsor for a fleet-side pilot, although budget ownership remains unverified.
What could block adoption?
If the problem belongs to the insurer, or current camera tools already solve it, there is no reason to buy.

02 / Operator and recipient

Adjuster or Claims Operations Lead

Interest
Complete evidence, less re-entry, faster fault decisions, subrogation, and fewer reopened files.
Influence
High. The claims team can reject a new intake path even when a fleet wants it.
Research question
Does video arrive as usable claim evidence or just another generic document?

03 / Technical gatekeeper

Camera and Claims-Platform Providers

Interest
Platform control, secure access, manageable support, and integrations that increase product value.
Influence
Very high. They set authentication, quota, format, API, marketplace, and partner requirements.
Research question
Can a new layer work through existing programs, or would the platforms make it redundant?

04 / Requirements setter

Defense Counsel and Special Investigations

Interest
A clear account of origin, copying, access, and preservation that can survive challenge.
Influence
High on requirements; lower on budget. Their standard may define what “trusted” must mean.
Legal boundary
Rule 902 certification addresses authentication only; relevance, hearsay, notice, and state rules still matter (Legal Information Institute, n.d.-b).

05 / Affected non-buyer

Drivers and Labor Representatives

Interest
Fair fault decisions, job security, clear camera policy, and limits on inward-facing recording.
Influence
Medium. Influence concentrates around consent, workplace policy, and trust.
Privacy boundary
California Penal Code § 632 concerns confidential communications and therefore can matter to in-cab audio; it is not a blanket road-video rule (California Legislative Information, 2017).

06 / Mission and opportunity

Roadside truth should arrive with its history intact.

A crash creates information at the edge of the fleet: video, time, location, vehicle identity, and the record of who handled it. Our mission is to help that information reach a fair commercial-auto claim decision without losing its context along the way.

The stakeholder map shows why this cannot be a fleet-only product. The fleet controls the source. Platforms control access. Claims teams decide whether the package fits their work. Counsel defines what must be preserved. Drivers live with the recording policy.

The opportunity is real only if interviews show all three

  1. 01A recurring failure—not a rare annoyance
  2. 02A person or organization that absorbs the cost
  3. 03A useful gap that current platforms do not already close

If customers say the footage arrives on time, current tools preserve enough context, or nobody owns the cost, we should change the problem rather than force the product.
